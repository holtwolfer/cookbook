Salmon Leek-henge

OneNote Seafood section · Original page dated Friday, November 1, 2019

Tags

Robert Irvinefishbasssalmonfancysaucedemi glace

Ingredients (first-pass extraction)

  • Sauce:
  • 3 Tbs olive oil
  • 2 white onions, sliced
  • 2 leeks, white only sliced
  • 2 shallots sliced
  • 1 red onion, sliced
  • 2 cups white wine
  • 3 cups stock
  • 1 cup veggie demi-glace
  • salt/pepper

Leeks

  • 4 medium to large potatoes
  • 6 leeks, 2.5 to 3 inch lengths
  • 1/2 bunch of chives, copped
  • 1 Tbs butter
  • salt/pepper
  • Fish:
  • 6 5oz pieces sea bass (salmon)
  • 4 Tbs olive oil
  • salt/pepper
  • 6 cipollini onions
  • garlic chives
  • thin slices of green leeks

minced basil

  • Sauce:
  • 2 cup chicken broth
  • 1 Tbs butter
  • soy sauce

Instructions (first-pass extraction)

  1. Preheat the oven to 375.
  2. Heat olive oil in a saute pan over medium heat. Cook the white onions, leeks, shallots, and red onions until translucent.
  3. Deglaze the pan with wine and reduce until almost dry. Add the stock, bring to a boil, reduce the heat and simmer until reduced by 3/4 to about 3/4 cup. Strain the sauce. Slowly whisk in demi-glace until the sauce is silky and coats the back of a spoon. Season with salt/pepper.

Leeks & Potatoes

  1. Drizzle the cipollini onions with oil and roast for about 20 minutes.
  2. Boil the potatoes and steam the leeks until just tender.
  3. Remove the center of the leeks by pushing from the bottom with a chopstick, leaving 1-2 outer layers.
  4. Mash the potatoes, mixing in the chives, butter, salt/pepper.
  5. The potatoes should be smooth enough for piping.
  6. Use a pastry bag to pipe the potatoes into the leeks.
  7. Fish:
  8. Pan-fry the fish in olive oil.

Demi-Glace Substitute

  1. 1 tsp cornstarch disolved in 3 TBS water
  2. 1/8 tsp balsamic vinegar or sherry
  3. Simmer broth & butter over med-low heat til reduced by half. Add cornstarch mixture stirring slowly. Simmer & reduce slightly again until smooth & rich. If too thick add vinegar & stir. Should not be gravy consistency.

Bok Choi (hash-like?)

  1. lime juice
